\section{Mechanism: Bounded vs.\ Unbounded Dual Behavior}
\label{app:mech}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bound}.}
Write $u=E/K\ge 0$. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} the first term $E/(E+K)=u/(1+u)$ increases from
$0$ to $1$ and the second $\rho\,u^2/(1+u^2)$ increases from $0$ to $\rho$, so
$0\le\ell<1+\rho$. Differentiating through $E=\max(0,S-K)$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:grad} for
$S>K$. There the first summand $K/(E+K)^2=\tfrac1K(1+u)^{-2}$ is at most $1/K$ (at $u{=}0$)
and decreasing; the second, $\tfrac{2\rho}{K}\,u/(1+u^2)^2$, is maximized at $u=1/\sqrt3$ with
value $\tfrac{2\rho}{K}\cdot\tfrac{9}{16\sqrt3}=\tfrac{9\rho}{8\sqrt3\,K}<\rho/K$ and tends to
$0$ as $u\to\infty$. Their sum therefore lies in $[0,(1+\rho)/K]$ and vanishes as
$E\to\infty$. Scaling $\ell$ and $\partial\ell/\partial S$ by a frozen multiplier $\lambda$
gives the bounds $\lambda(1+\rho)$ and $\lambda(1+\rho)/K$, neither depending on $E$.
$\square$

Why does a hard count cap stress the dual-ascent baselines? On the paper grid the
interaction is hidden: after the $50$-epoch warmup the classifier is already saturated,
and a saturation gate shared by every method (train accuracy $\ge 0.995$ on two
consecutive checks) stops cross-entropy updates within the first two constraint
epochs, so the constraint phase is pure count optimization for all methods alike. We
therefore probe with a one-epoch warmup, where the classifier keeps learning
throughout the constraint phase and the two objectives visibly compete
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}).

The probe shows three things. First, while the classifier is learning, the count cannot
be forced down: each epoch's cross-entropy updates re-inflate the constrained class, and
the excess oscillates around its starting level for ${\sim}60$ epochs under both
methods (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}c). Dual ascent responds exactly as designed: the
violation persists, so the multiplier grows every epoch, from $1$ to $53$ (seeds
$2$--$3$: $58$, $60$). In control terms this is integrator windup: the controller keeps
integrating an error it has no ability to act on. Second, the escalation buys nothing.
Every method takes a single
gradient-clipped constraint step per epoch (max-norm $1.0$, shared across methods), so
extra multiplier cannot buy extra descent: the two cross-entropy traces are numerically
indistinguishable (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}b), and the count comes down no faster at
$\lambda{=}53$ than at TraLO's ratcheted $\lambda\le 0.19$. The asymmetry is in what the
clip rail is doing. At $\lambda{=}53$ an unbounded-penalty gradient would dominate the
update by orders of magnitude were it not clipped, whereas TraLO's bounded penalty at
$\lambda\le 0.19$ demands orders of magnitude less force in the first place. Third, the
count falls only once the
classifier saturates and the gate stops CE updates (dotted lines in
Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}c), and then both methods reach the cap within a few
epochs. The count outcome is identical; what differs is the pressure applied to the
constrained class to get there.

The paper grid shows the same behavior in compressed form: the dual multipliers escalate
while TraLO's freezes ($\le 0.09$ there), Fioretto's native satisfaction is
${\sim}0.87$ ($0.84$ on OctMNIST and TissueMNIST, $0.93$ on DermMNIST), so its unbounded
multiplier stalls short of the cap on ${\sim}13\%$ of runs that never reach feasibility
within the budget (main paper Sec.~\ref{sec:conv}); runs that end
violated restore their best-satisfied checkpoint and remain competitive.

Nor is the escalation a step-size artifact. A dedicated fairness sweep (Block~C of
the released corpus; $96$ runs on the hard-regime cells) varies Fioretto's dual step
over $0.001$--$0.01$ and Hounie's $\eta$ over $0.001$--$0.1$: Fioretto's
constrained-class F1 moves by less than $0.001$ across the $10\times$ step range,
larger Hounie steps only hurt ($0.43\!\to\!0.35$), and no setting closes the gap to
TraLO on the OctMNIST tight cells (the best variant still trails by $0.019$ cc-F1).

We state this mechanism \emph{qualitatively only}. An audit shows that the
magnitude of the escalation does not predict where TraLO wins
(the multiplier is in fact largest in some cells where TraLO loses).
So the claim is ``dual ascent needs an unbounded multiplier plus a clip rail to hold a
hard count, while TraLO's bounded, frozen multiplier holds it alone,'' not any
dose--response of the form ``more escalation $\Rightarrow$ bigger win.''

\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_mechanism}
\caption{\textbf{Dual ascent winds up a multiplier the constraint cannot use: a
one-epoch-warmup probe} (DermMNIST $L50/G50$, RegNetY-400MF, seed~1).

the main grid (main paper Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence}; Sec.~\ref{app:mech} here). Each
method keeps one multiplier per constraint; the local caps are never violated on this
cell, so the local multipliers stay at zero and we plot the global-cap one. TraLO is
logged every $5$ epochs, hence its smoother-looking trace.}

\section{Complete Component Ablation}
\label{app:ablation}

A within-cell ablation isolates which ingredients carry the hard-regime advantage: on the
OctMNIST tight-cap cells where TraLO leads, we disable one component at a time and measure
the paired change in constrained-class F1 (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_complete};
$n{=}24$ per component). Two components are load-bearing.
Removing the optimizer reset at satisfaction costs $+0.079$ cc-F1, the single
largest effect, helping on all $24$ paired runs ($p\!=\!3\mathrm{e}{-7}$), because it breaks
the post-satisfaction descent momentum that would otherwise erode the constrained class.
Removing the undershoot hinge (the TraLO-bounded control, already in the frozen
grid) costs $+0.036$ cc-F1 ($p\!=\!3\mathrm{e}{-6}$; $0.019$--$0.070$ across cells). The other
components are cc-F1-neutral on these already-satisfied cells: the $\rho$ schedule and the
lambda freeze move it by under $0.001$ (n.s.). A dedicated KL sweep on TissueMNIST (the
$L30$ tight cap, MobileNetV3, $\alpha_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\in\!\{0,0.1,0.3,1.0\}$) confirms the
anchor is off for good reason: $\alpha_{\mathrm{KL}}{=}0$ (the paper recipe) gives the best
constrained-class \emph{and} macro-F1, while every nonzero anchor lowers cc-F1 (by
$0.01$--$0.03$) and only adds prediction edits. The KL term was a useful drift damper in an
early version of the method, before the optimizer reset and undershoot hinge; once those two
control post-satisfaction drift directly, the anchor is redundant. The advantage rests on
the reset and the hinge. The reset, $\rho$, and
freeze legs are a dedicated ablation under the identical frozen recipe; the hinge leg is
already in the main grid.

\section{Native Satisfaction: a Deployment Property, not a Headline}
\label{app:deploy}

\input{tables/tab_deploy}

We separate \emph{native satisfaction} (meeting the count with no post-hoc edit) from the
quality results in the main paper because, on its own, it does not discriminate methods: every
deployable system meets a hard count one way or another, and post-hoc clipping reaches
satisfaction $1.00$ by construction, simply by editing predictions. We report it only to
document the mechanism behind TraLO's quality advantage over clipping
(main paper Sec.~\ref{sec:deploy}): TraLO reaches the count during training and so never
invokes the editing step that costs clipping its accuracy. Table~\ref{tab:deploy} gives the
deployment costs at the tight $L30$ cap on MobileNetV3: macro-F1, accuracy, and the number of
predictions each method must overwrite (Flips). Figure~\ref{fig:deployment} shows native
satisfaction per method over the full grid. TraLO and Hounie-RCL meet the count in every
run; Fioretto-LDF and the hinge-free TraLO-bounded do most of the time; the post-hoc
clippers meet it natively only at loose caps. A
caveat on \emph{flips}: at near-trivial loose caps (e.g.\ OctMNIST $L80/L90$) TraLO can
record more flips than a clipper that barely has to act, so the
robust statement is ``satisfies natively while preserving macro-F1,'' never a raw flip count.

The macro-F1 advantage over clipping is not specific to MobileNetV3 or to the single $L30$ cap
of Table~\ref{tab:deploy}. Table~\ref{tab:deploybb} averages the paired gap over the symmetric
caps for each dataset--backbone cell, adding MobileNetV2 to the three main-grid backbones: the
mean gain is positive in every one of the twelve cells, largest on TissueMNIST, and it persists
under asymmetric caps (App.~\ref{app:regime}).

\section{Per-Cell Results Across the Full Grid}
\label{app:regime}

Tables~\ref{tab:gran_tissue}--\ref{tab:gran_asym} give the fully granular breakdown
behind the regime summary of the main paper (Sec.~\ref{sec:regime}): one row per
(dataset, backbone, cap), averaged over the four seeds \emph{only}, with no pooling across
backbones or caps. Each row pairs TraLO against the best constraint-trained baseline on
constrained-class F1 (per-seed best of Fioretto-LDF/Hounie-RCL) and against the best
post-hoc clipper on macro-F1, both paired by seed.

Two patterns read straight off the cells. The constrained-class win is
\emph{consistent} (all three backbones, every seed) only at the OctMNIST tight caps
($L30$/$L40$), where it is largest on ViT-B/16; a handful of isolated single-backbone
cells elsewhere also clear the $+0.005$ bar, but no other regime wins across backbones,
and none is a loss beyond seed noise. The macro-F1 margin over clipping is positive in
$77$ of the $81$ symmetric cells; the four exceptions are the very tightest $L10$/$L20$
caps, where the cap nearly zeroes out the constrained class and the gap is within
$\pm0.02$. The asymmetric caps (Table~\ref{tab:gran_asym}) hold the
\emph{preliminary} constrained-class win, on DermMNIST where the global cap is tighter
than the group cap. That per-cell table is MobileNetV3, the only backbone with the full
asymmetric grid; a cross-backbone check on the four shared $G{<}L$ cap pairs
(Table~\ref{tab:asym_xbackbone}) shows the constrained-class win reproduces on RegNet
($+0.9$pp, $75\%$ seed-winrate) and ties on the ViT ($-0.0$pp), while the macro-F1
advantage over clipping holds on all three backbones. We report the asymmetric result as
preliminary because only MobileNetV3 has been run on the complete asymmetric grid, not
because a single backbone shows it.

Table~\ref{tab:bbgen} summarizes the constrained-class picture across backbones, adding
MobileNetV2 to the three main-grid backbones and averaging the paired gap over the symmetric
caps: on TissueMNIST and DermMNIST no backbone shows a systematic advantage (means within
$\pm0.003$), while OctMNIST is positive on all four. The honest tie and the OctMNIST win are
each backbone-general, not artifacts of one architecture.
