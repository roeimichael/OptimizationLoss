\begin{abstract}
Completed studies support a bounded development-set finding for a
snapshot side-copy intervention on knee radiographs. They do not
establish that training-time dual-constraint TraLO beats a matched
post-hoc clipper without secondary tradeoffs or on untouched data.
This is a reproducibility and claim-readiness report, not held-out confirmation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and intervention}
The maintained question is whether training-time TraLO improves
deployed constrained-class F1 (cc-F1) over a matched post-hoc clipper
without being dominated on the rest of the profile. The positive knee
study instead trains the base model once per seed, applies a targeted
constraint step to side copies of saved epoch models, and averages
their probabilities. Controls are an unstepped snapshot ensemble and
a same-radius random-direction sham. All use the same capped-first
allocator with 76 grade-3 slots. This is a side-copy intervention,
not a training-time dual-constraint loss result.

These prespecified E2 contrasts use a two-contrast Holm family within
each backbone. ResNet18 and RegNetY also lost weighted-F1 against PTO:
$-0.25$ $[-0.48,-0.03]$ and $-0.52$ $[-0.84,-0.20]$
percentage points. Thus their cc-F1 gains are not all-metric wins;
B5 reverses the primary effect. All knee outcomes are on an inspected
development pool. Seed intervals do not measure variation over unseen
patients or sites. The 1,656-image Chen test remains unopened.

The fMoW cap-10 TraLO-minus-focal-clipper class-conditional F1 was
$-0.01393$ with interval $[-0.01964,-0.00821]$ on viewed development
countries. The reserved countries remain unopened.

For the calibrated four-seed MobileNetV3 cells, allocated
TraLO-minus-PTO cc-F1 was:
\begin{itemize}
\item ISIC 2020 level 1: $-0.0061$ (CI $-0.0707$ to $+0.0586$).
\item ISIC 2020 level 2: $+0.0365$ (CI $-0.0496$ to $+0.1225$).
\item CelebA level 1: $+0.000045$ (CI $-0.000140$ to $+0.000230$).
\item CelebA level 2: $+0.000079$ (CI $-0.000951$ to $+0.001110$).
\end{itemize}
Neither exploratory cell demonstrates a positive local
dual-constraint effect.

\section{Code and artifact validation}
Release \path{0d90bc6bff90fdf7555f19c00061e0e5b0426147}
passed the complete local suite (646 passed, one skipped, 42 subtests)
and focused CPU tests on both DSI hosts with GPUs hidden. Git identity
and committed SHA-256 bytes match the immutable release on both hosts.
CPU replay of all four complete knee blocks reproduced every archived
outcome line exactly; the four error logs were empty. A separate
read-only implementation at release \path{5f74ce387a48cb22e69742c3d8db71a4ead95c62}
recomputed capped-slot allocation, class metrics, paired intervals and
Holm correction from the saved snapshot tensors. Its expanded local suite
passed (649 passed, one skipped); focused CPU tests passed on dsisco01.
Direct dsisco02 access was unavailable for this second release. An
off-host comparison checked 96 summary fields and 792 per-seed cc-F1
values against archived reports within published rounding, including
the B5 reversal and weighted-F1 harms. This is
arithmetic validation on the same development pool, not new evaluation.

The old snapshot pilot gate called a helper that internally read
development labels and computed metrics, although it printed no pilot
score. We found no evidence of pilot-quality-based tuning, but the old
gate was not technically label-blind. The new release separates the
integrity gate from label-based scoring, detects a corrupt side snapshot,
and checks every fixed seed before opening labels. Historical runs and
results remain unchanged.

\section{Open gates}
A stronger paper claim needs a frozen training-time method, matched
controls, allocator, caps, primary contrast, secondary tradeoff rule,
and analysis plan before sealed labels are opened. Opening the Chen
test or reserved countries requires a user decision under project rules.
The proposed HAM10000 melanoma-versus-rest cell changes the scientific
question and is not approved; patient IDs are unavailable. ISIC 2024
still lacks a frozen patient-disjoint split and several data and
training gates. The verified nonoverlapping compute spend is at least
72.175499 GPU-hours of a finite 96-hour ceiling; 23.824501 hours is
only an upper bound on unspent budget, not a certified balance.
