## 0. What this dossier is for

Two findings, each independently confirmed by more than one route, bear on the paper's
central contribution. Both are fixable. Neither requires starting over. But both change
what the paper claims, so they are presented as evidence for a decision rather than
applied unilaterally.

Everything below marked **CONFIRMED** was recomputed from raw data by at least two
independent implementations (an auditor's and mine), or read directly out of the source.

**First, the good news, because it frames everything else.** The paper's arithmetic is
sound. Across the audit, ~90 headline numbers were recomputed from the released corpus
and the raw runs:

- Table 1: **324/324** values and **216/216** bold/underline decisions reproduce exactly.
- The three granular appendix tables: **486/486** values plus all block headers reproduce.
- The entire graft appendix, the penalty-shape negative result, the deployment table,
  the satisfaction census, the LOBO cross-validation, the analytic bounds in App. A,
  and every dataset fact: all reproduce exactly.
- The headline grid is **exactly 1944 runs**, 486 cells at exactly 4 seeds each, zero
  duplicate keys, zero NaN metrics, zero satisfaction/excess contradictions.
- The "frozen recipe" in §4 matches the actual run configs value for value.
- **No undisclosed test-label leakage.** Caps are derived from true test counts, which
  the paper discloses twice and correctly frames as an exogenous deployment capacity.

The problems are not sloppiness. They are two places where a control did not do what it
was documented to do, and the paper reported the result as though it had.

## FINDING 1 — The OctMNIST tight-cap win measures budget utilisation, not classification quality

**Severity: CRITICAL · CONFIRMED by four independent routes**

### 1.1 What the paper claims

> §5.1: "at the OctMNIST tight caps (L30/L40) the package leads the best
> constraint-trained baseline on every backbone, by a mean cc-F1 gap of **+0.038**
> across the six cells (sign test p=0.031), peaking at **+0.081** on the ViT-B/16."

This is the paper's one backbone-general cc-F1 result. The ablation and graft in §6 are
measured on these same six cells.

### 1.2 The mechanism

`src/pipeline/eval.py:47` calls `targeted_correction(..., force_exact=True)`. That
function's own docstring states its purpose:

> "force_exact=True (default): always run all phases — push count to **exactly K**
> so cross-method comparisons are **apples-to-apples** (drop over, fill under)."

So the pipeline is *designed* to equalise every method's spent budget before the
comparison. It does so successfully at 7 of the 9 cap levels. It fails at exactly
L30 and L40 on OctMNIST — the six headline cells.

**The cause** (`src/utils/posthoc_adjustment.py`): `_round_to_K` rounds each cap
independently, so on OctMNIST's group counts (79/89/82) the local caps sum to more than
the global cap at L30 (76 > 75) and L40 (101 > 100), and nowhere else. Phase 2 fills the
global count to K; Phase 3b then fills each group to its own local cap — and Phase 3b
checks the *source* class's global gap (`global_gap.get(old_class,0) <= 0`) but never
checks whether adding to target class `c` breaches `c`'s own global cap. Phase 2 has the
mirror-image local guard; Phase 3b has no global guard. The count lands at K+1,
`_check_all_satisfied` fails, and the LP fallback — whose objective is to maximise
probability mass subject to ≤ caps — reverts the entire fill, because the raw argmax was
already feasible and probability-maximal.

Because the fill is reverted rather than partially applied, `compute_flips(raw, final)`
returns 0, which is why the corpus reports `flips = 0` and the failure was invisible.

### 1.3 The evidence

**Final constrained-class count as a percentage of the cap K, OctMNIST, `paper_final`:**

| cap | Fioretto | Hounie | TraLO | TraLO-bnd | Heuristic | LP-clip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L10 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| L20 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| **L30** | **83.2** | **68.6** | **97.9** | **84.1** | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| **L40** | **88.2** | **77.6** | **97.9** | **88.6** | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| L50 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 99.9 |
| L60–L90 | ~99–100 | ~99–100 | ~99–100 | ~99–100 | ~99–100 | 96.2→73.1 |

The equalisation works everywhere except the two rows carrying the win.

**Consequence.** Re-scoring every method's own probabilities through the same top-K
allocator the clippers already receive (no retraining; exactly what `force_exact=True`
was written to do):

| protocol | mean cell gap | seed wins | cells > 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **As-run** (what the paper reports) | **+0.0377** | 24/24 | 6/6 |
| **Budget-equalised** (what the code intended) | **−0.0016** | 7/24 | 2/6 |

Per cell, equalised: MNV3 L30 −0.011, MNV3 L40 +0.004, RegNet L30 +0.003,
RegNet L40 −0.003, ViT L30 −0.003, ViT L40 +0.000. The ViT's headline +0.081 becomes
−0.003.

**Four independent routes agree:**
1. Code auditor, re-running the allocator: +0.038 → −0.0003.
2. My own independent implementation: +0.0377 → −0.0016.
3. Track B auditor, decomposing differently — re-scoring each dual at TraLO's predicted
   count using *the dual's own precision* — finds the residual gap **negative in 12 of 12
   contrasts**, with quota-fill accounting for **103–169% (median 117%)** of the gap.
   That route also finds TraLO's precision on its own selected set is **0.021 lower**
   than the duals'.
4. Convergent external evidence: the win dies at native resolution (where everyone fills
   the cap), dies at L10/L15 (ceiling crush, `b9_octtight`, 48 runs, all p ≥ 0.39), and
   never appears against the post-hoc clipper.

### 1.4 The part that is *not* a problem — and is a stronger result

The underlying property is real, and it is **larger than the paper claims**. Native
(pre-post-hoc) budget utilisation, across the whole grid:

| dataset | TraLO | Fioretto | Hounie |
|---|---|---|---|
| OctMNIST | 75.5–98.6% | 72.4–90.3% | 58.0–89.6% |
| DermMNIST | 87.9–98.2% | 64.8–90.2% | 61.0–91.7% |
| TissueMNIST | 82.4–94.0% | 74.5–90.7% | 77.7–89.4% |

**TraLO lands closer to its budget than either dual in all 27 cap × dataset
combinations.** The duals systematically leave 10–40% of the cap unspent, and unspent
budget is forgone recall on the constrained class.

So the phenomenon is grid-wide. What is confined to L30/L40 is only where the broken
fill *failed to mask it*. Elsewhere the fill equalised everyone to 100% and the property
became invisible — and was reported as a tie.

**The honest reading:** the reset and hinge produce native budget utilisation that dual
ascent does not achieve; a free post-hoc top-K fill achieves the same cc-F1. That is a
convergence/feasibility contribution, not a constrained-class quality contribution — and
it is precisely the story §5.4 and App. C already tell. The paper's own §5.1 text already
states the duals "finish 11–36% under the budget… so recall falls with the unspent
budget." It simply does not take the last step.

### 1.5 What this does *not* touch

- The macro-F1 advantage over post-hoc clipping (+0.053 / +0.016 / +0.021). It survives
  equalisation (+0.020 at these cells) because it comes from off-class damage, not budget.
- The tie regions.
- The penalty-shape negative result.
- The convergence result.
- The native-satisfaction result.

## FINDING 2 — One of the paper's two named components is never isolated, and alone it is harmful

**Severity: CRITICAL · CONFIRMED**

### 2.1 The contradiction

- §6, line 809: "we disable **one component at a time**."
- §6, line 815: "Removing the undershoot hinge (**the TraLO-bounded control**) costs +0.036."
- §4, lines 498–501: TraLO-bounded "strips **both** load-bearing additions, the optimizer
  reset and the undershoot hinge."

The hinge row is therefore *reset + hinge vs neither*, not an isolated hinge ablation.

### 2.2 The evidence

The leave-one-out ablation (`extra_robustness_corpus.csv`, `B_loo_ablation`) contains
exactly four arms: `plus_kl`, `no_rho`, `no_reset`, `no_freeze`. **There is no
`no_hinge` arm anywhere in the corpus.**

OctMNIST tight caps (L30+L40, 3 backbones × 4 seeds), mean cc-F1:

| arm | reset | hinge | cc-F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| TraLO | ✓ | ✓ | 0.4552 |
| `no_reset` | ✗ | ✓ | **0.3761** |
| TraLO-bounded | ✗ | ✗ | 0.4188 |
| `+KL` | ✓ | ✓ (+KL) | **0.4655** |

- Paper's "Adam reset +0.079" = 0.4552 − 0.3761 = **+0.0791** ✓ reproduces
- Paper's "Undershoot hinge +0.036" = 0.4552 − 0.4188 = **+0.0364** ✓ reproduces
- **Hinge alone vs neither = 0.3761 − 0.4188 = −0.0427**

The ordering is impossible under the paper's own labels: removing *both* components costs
less (+0.036) than removing the reset alone (+0.079). The reason is that the hinge without
the reset is **actively harmful**.

### 2.3 Why it matters

"Two Portable Components" is the title, the abstract, and the central claim. The data
support: *the reset is load-bearing, and the reset+hinge pair beats neither.* They do not
support an independently portable hinge. The graft experiment does support **pair**
portability (Fioretto 6/6, Hounie 5/6), so "the pair transfers" stands.

### 2.4 Cost to settle it

A genuine `no_hinge` arm is TraLO with `hybrid_mode: bounded_only`,
`reset_optimizer_at_sat: true`. The code path already exists
(`VALID_MODES = ("bounded_only", "undershoot_hinge")`). **24 runs, ~3 min each — under
2 GPU-hours.** This is the single cheapest, highest-value experiment available.

## FINDING 3 — An ablation row contradicting a paper claim was dropped from its own table

**Severity: MAJOR · CONFIRMED**

`paper/data/corpus/ablation_complete.csv` contains **five** rows. `tab_ablation_complete.tex`
prints **four**. The omitted row:

```
+KL,24,-0.0103,0.208,-0.0477,0.0431,3.98e-02
```

On the six headline cells, **adding** the KL anchor *improves* cc-F1 by **+0.0103**
(p = 0.040, TraLO wins only 21% of paired runs). It is the **best-performing arm in the
entire ablation** (0.4655 vs TraLO's 0.4552).

App. B nevertheless states the anchor is off "for good reason: α_KL=0 gives the best
constrained-class *and* macro-F1." That claim rests solely on `kl_sweep.csv`, which is
TissueMNIST/L30/MobileNetV3 only, has an **empty seed column**, contains only **two
distinct values per arm duplicated to look like 4–8 rows** (real n = 2, from 8 runs on
disk), and has **no macro-F1 column at all**.

The dropped row has a larger and more significant effect than two rows that *are*
printed (ρ: +0.0007, p=0.74; freeze: +0.0004, p=0.65).

**Fix:** restore the row; rewrite the paragraph as "KL hurts on TissueMNIST L30 but helps
modestly on the OctMNIST tight cells; we keep α_KL=0 for simplicity, not because it
dominates."

## FINDING 4 — The convergence census is contaminated with a different study

**Severity: CRITICAL · CONFIRMED**

`paper/data/corpus/convergence_census.csv`: **60 of 216 rows (27.8%)** — all 10
MobileNetV3 cells — carry `sat_epoch` values that exist nowhere in the canonical corpus
and match only the `group_column='sex'` study (`paperv2_phase5`), a different constraint
problem. The CSV has no `group_column` column, so the mix is invisible.

Worked example: census line 20 = `dermmnist,MobileNetV3,30,hounie_rcl,3,50,True,60.0`.
The only run anywhere with `Satisfaction Epoch = 60` for that key is
`paperv2_phase5/L30_G30/hounie_rcl/seed_3`, whose `group_column` is `"sex"`. The
canonical runs give 46 and 47.

This is the *same* pooling defect the project previously identified and fixed in the
table generators — it survives here. The 8 RegNet cells are clean. The census also mixes
6 campaigns and 2 code revisions, and draws **zero** rows from `paper_final`.

§5.4 is designated a core contribution, so this needs rebuilding from
`paper_final` + `paper_backbones` through the canonical loader. Mitigating: the
contamination is method-balanced, so the *direction* of the convergence result will very
likely survive; the printed medians will move (cell-level aggregation gives
16.75/18.5/43.5 rather than the printed 17/20/49.5).

## FINDING 5 — Two factual errors in the description of the data

**Severity: MAJOR · CONFIRMED**

**5a. "All three are 28×28 images upsampled to 224×224" (§4:465, repeated at §7:880 and
in Broader Impact) is false for DermMNIST.** Measured directly against the raw HAM10000
JPEGs: normalized residual after 28px reconstruction is 0.0507 for the project's derm
data vs 0.0404 for a genuine-224 reference and 0.0114 for a forced-28px control; the
FFT high-frequency fraction matches the genuine reference (0.00168 vs 0.00130) and is
17× the control. The derm data is additionally **byte-identical** to
`dermamnist_corrected_224.npz`. OctMNIST and TissueMNIST *are* genuine 28px upsamples.

This also partly self-contradicts the Limitations paragraph "we do not know how the reset
and hinge transfer to native-resolution medical imaging" — DermMNIST **is** native-
resolution medical imaging and is already in the paper.

**5b. "with their official splits" (§4:457) is false for all three.** Actual vs official:

| dataset | this paper | official MedMNIST v2 |
|---|---|---|
| OctMNIST | 12,000 / 1,000 | 97,477 / 1,000 |
| TissueMNIST | 9,600 / **2,400** | 165,466 / **47,280** |
| DermMNIST | 8,012 / 2,003 | 7,007 / 2,005 |

Train+val are merged and both splits stratified-subsampled. App. F already quotes the
subsampled counts (171/2400, 223/2003), contradicting §4. The test set is what the
constraint acts on, so its size is not cosmetic.

## FINDING 6 — The multiple-comparisons paragraph is arithmetically incoherent

**Severity: MAJOR · CONFIRMED**

§4:583–590 claims a family of "twelve cell-by-component comparisons" and computes
"q = 0.05 × 6/12 = 0.025 for the smallest p-value and q = 0.05 × 12/12 = 0.05 for the
largest."

Benjamini–Hochberg's rank-*i* threshold is `q·i/m`. With m=12 and two p-values at ranks
1 and 2, the thresholds are **0.0042 and 0.0083** — neither component survives. The
arithmetic actually printed is the **m=2** computation with numerator and denominator
each multiplied by 6. The sentence is also self-contradictory: it says both values lie
under their thresholds, but 0.031 > its own stated 0.025.

The conclusion happens to hold at m=2. The derivation as printed does not. **Fix:** state
m=2 with thresholds 0.025/0.05, or delete the paragraph and lean on effect sizes and the
LOBO cross-validation, which reproduce exactly and are the genuinely strong evidence.

## 9. Options

**On Finding 1 (the headline).**

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Report both protocols; promote budget utilisation to the finding.** Show cc-F1 as-run *and* budget-equalised, and lead with the grid-wide native-utilisation result (27/27 cells). | Rewrites §5.1 and parts of §6–7. The two-components story survives and gains *broader* support. Most honest; arguably the strongest available paper. |
| **B** | **Fix the bug, re-evaluate consistently, report the tie.** | cc-F1 becomes an honest tie everywhere; the paper leans on macro-F1-over-clipping, native satisfaction, and convergence. Most conservative; loses the cc-F1 story. |
| **C** | **Keep the as-run protocol and disclose it prominently.** | Requires stating that the cc-F1 comparison is not budget-equalised and that a top-K fill erases it — which effectively retracts the headline anyway. |

Recommended: **A**. It is the only option where the paper ends up claiming something
both true and larger than what it claims now.

**On Finding 2.** Run the 24-run `no_hinge` arm (< 2 GPU-hours). Either it isolates the
hinge and the title is vindicated, or it confirms the interaction and the claim becomes
"a reset-plus-hinge pair, with the reset dominant." Either way the ambiguity ends cheaply.

**Note on re-evaluation cost.** Findings 1 and 4 need **no GPU training** — they are
re-runs of the evaluation step on saved predictions, seconds per run.

## 10. Bottom line

The paper is honest in intent and almost entirely correct in arithmetic. Its authors
disclosed the post-hoc threshold, the motivation–result mismatch, the naming caveat, and
a negative result about their own formulation — that is unusually good practice, and it
is why the remaining problems are visible at all.

Two controls did not do what they were documented to do, and the paper reported the
outputs as though they had. The corrections point toward a *different and broader*
contribution than the one currently claimed, not toward a smaller one. Nothing here
requires starting over.
