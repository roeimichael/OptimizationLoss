# HAM10000 prospective design for review, 4 October 2026

**Status: proposal only. No split, target, cap, seed, or GPU booking is frozen or authorized by this document.** It builds on the source audit in `experiments/ham10000_source_gate_20261004.md`. The earlier 70/15/15 candidate and its failed local-group support check remain part of the record.

The concrete option is to test the existing dual-constraint mechanism on the official ISIC 2018 Task 3 HAM10000 images with a **melanoma-versus-all-other-diagnoses** target. This is melanoma detection, not a general malignant-versus-benign target: the negative pool includes BCC and AKIEC. It would retain the existing image-only classifier and use the recorded sex attribute only to index the pooled and male/female local allocation ceilings. Missing-sex examples (47 in the full source) would remain eligible for the global ceiling but have no local sex ceiling, as in the existing ISIC 2020 policy. All seven original diagnoses, confirmation methods, and missing-sex counts would be reported by split; weaker negative-label confirmation must not be hidden.

The single prospective alternative to the unsupported 70/15/15 candidate is a lesion-disjoint 60/10/30 train/stop/development split using the project's first-eight-byte SHA-256 partition rule and namespace `tralo-ham10000-lesion-alt60-10-30-20261004`. It has 6,028/995/2,992 images and 4,498/734/2,238 lesions, with 653/133/327 melanoma labels respectively. The development portion has 1,392 female, 1,582 male, and 18 missing-sex images. On these *label-free groups*, the unchanged ISIC 2020 quota function passes its 1,000-image local-support gate and produces level-1 female/male caps 21/24 and global cap 30, or level-2 caps 42/48 and global cap 60. The former 70/15/15 candidate failed the same gate with female 676 and male 833; this proposal does not change the threshold or erase that result.

These are metadata feasibility checks, not training evidence or a reason to select a dose. Patient IDs are unavailable, so the split cannot guarantee patient independence. Its larger development fraction reduces the training pool and changes the experimental question. Original-JPEG hashes and IDs do not overlap the frozen ISIC 2020 source, but resized/re-encoded or semantic duplication is still possible. No development model score has been used to define this option.

If this scientific design is selected, the next stage must freeze the split, target, missing-group rule, allocator, backbone, schedule, method arms, primary contrast and finite cost plan before any development-quality observation. It must then pass data-file and duplicate checks on the prepared arrays, finite task and constraint gradients on actual images, null/sham and allocator parity, correction-dose/logging checks, local/server byte-identical source tests, and an independent label-blind pilot gate. The global 96 aggregate GPU-hour account currently has only a **72.175499-hour nonoverlapping lower bound** of use, leaving **at most 23.824501 hours**, not a certified spendable balance; outstanding smokes and other jobs must be reconciled. Only after those gates and an exclusive two-host physical-GPU inventory could a new pilot be claimed. A pilot would be a validity and headroom check, not a quality-selection run; fixed matched seeds would require a measured projection under a finite cell budget.

The decision for review is whether this **distinct melanoma-detection, lesion-disjoint but not patient-disjoint** dataset cell is scientifically acceptable under the stated label-provenance and training-fraction limits. If not, keep HAM as an audited but unrun source and continue data-readiness work elsewhere. Neither choice changes the completed fMoW, ISIC 2020, or CelebA results.
